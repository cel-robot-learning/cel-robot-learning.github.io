01 / Introduction

Collective Experience Learning

Collective Experience Learning improves a shared task policy using autonomous rollouts from a robot fleet. After initialization with demonstrations and human corrections, Failure-guided Experience Retrieval Fine-Tuning (FERT) trains on successful trajectories and uses failure contexts to select relevant experience. During FERT, operators provide binary task-outcome labels.

Across five separately trained manipulation tasks, FERT achieves 93.3–99.2% success on held-out robots. On Phone Packaging, post-training with 96 robots raises success from 79.2% to 98.3%.

Robot Post-training from Real-world Experience

Object placement, contact conditions and robot variation can cause errors to accumulate during multi-stage tasks. Human interventions supply corrective actions for these states. As more robots run concurrently, experts must monitor more interaction streams and provide more corrections. Collective Experience Learning uses fleet-generated trajectories and binary outcome labels for subsequent policy updates.

02 / Task Setup

Tasks and robot setup

Each task uses a separately trained policy on AgiBot G2 robots. Observations combine one head-camera view, two wrist-camera views and proprioceptive state. Actions are delta joint positions for both arms.

03 / Collective Experience Learning

Failure-guided Experience Retrieval Fine-Tuning (FERT)

FERT stores successful rollouts in a shared buffer. Visual features and robot state encode failure contexts, which are matched to successful sequences using subsequence dynamic time warping. Retrieved intervals receive higher sampling priority during supervised fine-tuning.

04 / Experimental Results

Experimental Results

Each checkpoint is evaluated on two robots excluded from training data collection, with 60 trials per robot. Task configurations match training. Error bars show 95% Wilson confidence intervals.

Scaling with fleet size

Phone Packaging reaches 50.8%, 86.7% and 98.3% success with 4, 16 and 96 FERT training robots, respectively, compared with 79.2% at initialization. Across all five tasks, FERT success increases over the reported fleet sizes. Per-robot collection time is fixed within each task and method, so robot count and total experience increase together.

Contribution of failure-guided retrieval

With 16 training robots on Phone Packaging, full FERT achieves 86.7% success. Removing failure-guided retrieval lowers success to 64.2%, a decrease of 22.5 percentage points.

Removing the one-second extensions around each retrieved segment reduces success from 86.7% to 57.5%.

Ablation studies

Priority decay factors of 0.80, 0.88 and 0.95 yield 70.8%, 86.7% and 82.5% success, respectively. Synchronizing policy weights every 100, 500 and 1,500 optimizer steps yields 75.0%, 86.7% and 80.0%. All configurations use Phone Packaging with 16 training robots and 120 evaluation trials.

Evaluation protocol

For each task, FERT, RLT and RECAP start from the same selected multi-robot HG-DAgger checkpoint. Phone Charging, Stool Assembly and Phone Packaging use mixed multi-robot HG-DAgger data. RLT uses single-robot online reinforcement learning; RECAP uses offline reinforcement learning on fleet data. Both baselines use human corrections during post-training. Robot and training budgets follow the method-specific configurations in the manuscript.

05 / Infrastructure

Fault-tolerant Asynchronous Training System (FATS)

FATS separates robot interaction, policy inference and optimization into independent workers. Interaction and inference run at the edge; training runs in the cloud. HotSwarm manages workers joining, leaving and recovering, while unaffected workers continue collecting experience.

Fleet activity and local interruptions

During a 96-robot run from approximately 10:30 to 18:30, 22 robots recorded at least one system error. Collection resumed after many error events. Blank intervals in the trial timeline include environment resets and operator breaks. Physical repair and resets require operator involvement.

Network topology

Two tier-1 switches and three tier-2 switches connect the laboratory resources, with dual 100 Gbps links between interconnected switches. A dedicated 30 Gbps edge–cloud connection carries trajectory uploads and policy updates. NAS provides edge storage; GPFS provides shared cloud storage.

06 / Conclusion

Conclusion

The experiments train one policy per task and evaluate it on held-out instances of the same robot platform under matching task configurations. FERT requires an initialization that can generate successful rollouts, binary outcome labels and physical support for resets and maintenance.
